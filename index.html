I am a Ph.D. candidate in Applied Mathematics and Computational Science at the University of Pennsylvania. My research brings together medical imaging, computational neuroscience, statistical modeling, and biomedical artificial intelligence. Outside research, I have run varsity track and completed multiple half marathons.

My doctoral work integrates structural and diffusion MRI, tractography, brain-network representations, and longitudinal mixed-effects models to study gray- and white-matter degeneration. I am also interested in multimodal AI and foundation models for precision medicine.

I develop scientific software as part of this research. At Case Western Reserve University, I developed Quick Annotator, a deep-learning-assisted digital pathology annotation tool, and contributed to HistoQC and C++ plugins for Sedeen Viewer. Since fall 2026, I have contributed to agentic AI coding and application prototyping with the Wharton Research Data Services (WRDS) engineering team.

Quantitative research and projects

My master’s research studied volatility modeling with Lévy processes and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck models in the context of option pricing. I am interested in bringing empirical research, statistical modeling, and scientific computing to quantitative investment research.

My quantitative projects reflect my experience with quantitative research and trading workflows, from studying market activity to testing signals, simulating portfolios, and forecasting volatility.

  • PCR Market Watch tracks U.S. options activity using put/call ratios and historical comparisons.
  • Alpha Lab tests equity signals and simulates portfolio allocation and order execution. Its companion Alpha Training Lab walks through the research workflow.
  • Lévy Volatility Lab compares volatility forecasts for USD/JPY and U.S. equities. Open the demo on Hugging Face.
